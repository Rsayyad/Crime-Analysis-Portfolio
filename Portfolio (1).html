About

I'm Raneem Sayyad, a criminal justice researcher and analyst based in Ashburn, VA. I'm finishing my M.S. in Criminal Justice at George Mason University (Expected May 2026) with a perfect 4.0 GPA and the Academic Excellence Award. Before that I earned my B.A. in Criminology, Law & Society — Cum Laude, Dean's List seven times over.

My work sits at the intersection of data science and public safety. I translate raw law enforcement records into clear, actionable intelligence: spatial hotspot maps that show where crime clusters, ARIMA and neural network forecasts that anticipate when it will spike, and statistical analyses that surface the patterns hidden inside FBI UCR, NFLIS, and calls-for-service datasets.

I also volunteer as a Case Screener at the Mid-Atlantic Innocence Project, where I review criminal case records for wrongful conviction claims and conduct preliminary legal research — a reminder that rigorous analysis has real stakes for real people.

I bring this same precision and care to every role I step into, whether that's a research environment, a policy team, a law enforcement agency, or the intelligence community. If you work with data and want to do something meaningful with it, I'd love to talk.
